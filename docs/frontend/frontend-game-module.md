## Overview

The Game page (`/game`) is the real-time gameplay screen. It has:

1. **Ludo board**: drawn by the `Board` component, with the four colored tracks, safe stars, clash-chain badges, and Wall Breaker pips.
2. **Dice roller**: the `Die` component, which animates the roll and shows 1-6.
3. **Piece interaction**: pieces drawn on the board; you can click them when it is your turn, including the armed Wall Breaker exit.
4. **Waiting room and room rules**: the PvP READY/seat screen and the host-only room-rule rows — clash, the mid-clash key shift it owns, free exit, the hardcore no-safe-zones rule, then the cosmetic attack-animations toggle last.
5. **Clash QTE**: `ClashOverlay`, shared by keyboard and touch, with the result-card release handshake.
6. **Rules and results**: the ten-page rules deck in `CyberModal`, opened by the GAME RULES button under the board, and `ResultsModal` for every end path.
7. **Real-time engine connection**: connects to the Ludo Engine through Socket.IO (`connectSocket`), using the match token from `activeMatch`.
8. **Controls and settings**: the CONTROLS & SETTINGS button under the board opens a 340 px popover — the three key rows (dice, select piece, goal count), the auto-close row, the language and theme switches, and the retro audio switch, which sits outside the scrolling body.
9. **The room's channel** (M3): `ChatCard` in the left column, under the roster, in PvP rooms only. It is the private channel by name as well as by construction (20.3): `# ENCRYPTED COMMS` in the expanded card's head, `ENCRYPTED COMMS` on the compact plate, against the lobby's public `# HYPERSPACE COMMS`. The slot shows the newest three lines; pressing it opens the full card as an overlay (transcript, composer with a `n / 300` counter, one reserved status band). The transcript is `useChatFeed`'s (19.8), the same hook the lobby's public channel drives. While the room is still `WAITING`, `InviteComms` is offered as the card's `sidePanel`: the friends who are online and **not** already seated here, portaled beside the card, off until the card head's switch (beside CLOSE) asks for it, challenging through `useChallenges` with the room's own `gameId` (`POST /api/friends/:friendId/invite`) and greying from the server's sent list. The panel unmounts at the start; the card does not. A clash leaves the card mounted and readable but takes every key (`inert`). Pressing a pilot's name in the transcript opens `ChatNameCard` (19.7), the card the lobby opens too, with the duel left out.

> **Note:** The Game page is fully real-time. It connects to the engine on the page's own origin (`/socket.io/`) and sends `join_game`, `roll_dice`, `move_piece`, `skip_wall_breaker`, `arm_wall_breaker`, `clash_input`, `clash_card_hold`, `update_modifiers`, `player_ready`, `chat:send` and `end_game`. It renders `game_joined`, `dice_rolled`, `piece_moved`, `turn_skipped`, `clash_*`, `modifiers_updated`, `chat:message`, `chat:history`, `game_ended` and the presence/lobby frames. Game state is dispatched into `game/reducer.ts`; see `socket.ts` for the complete event contract. The seat's colour is not chosen here: it is claimed on the Lobby screen and arrives with the join.

**Guest mode.** When `activeMatch.isGuest` is true, the page joins under the localized `guest.playerName`, sends both exits to `/login`, and passes the guest props to `ResultsModal`. **Play again** posts the stored `guestSetup` to `/api/match/guest` and navigates to `/game?gameId=<new id>`, which remounts the page because `App` keys the route on `gameId`. A `GUEST_LIMITED` or `GUEST_CAPACITY` refusal is shown on the end card, which stays open. See [`../guest-mode.md`](../guest-mode.md).

## Core Logic / Flow

### 1. Engine Connection & Join

Sequence of steps when the Game page mounts with an active match.
```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant Game as Game page
    participant Socket as Game socket
    participant Engine as ludo-engine

    Game->>Socket: Connect using the game token
    Socket->>Engine: Connect
    Game->>Socket: join_game (gameId, color)
    Engine-->>Game: game_joined (the board)
    Game->>Game: Draw the board and pieces
```

### 2. Dice Roll

Sequence of steps when the player clicks the die. A second tap on the spinning die
brakes it: the roll lands somewhere between 450 ms and its natural 750 ms window.
```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant User
    participant Game as Game page
    participant Socket as Game socket
    participant Engine as ludo-engine

    User->>Game: Click the die (or the Roll button)
    Game->>Socket: roll_dice
    Game->>Game: Tumble starts, brake window opens
    Engine->>Engine: Roll + work out legal moves
    Engine-->>Game: dice_rolled (value + movable pieces)
    opt Die tapped again, 200 ms or later
        User->>Game: Tap the spinning die
        Game->>Game: Brake: land between 450 ms and 750 ms
    end
    Game->>Game: Land on the server's face, highlight which pieces can move
```

### 3. Piece Movement

Sequence of steps after the player clicks a movable piece.
```mermaid
sequenceDiagram
    participant User
    participant Game as Game page
    participant Socket as Game socket
    participant Engine as ludo-engine

    User->>Game: Click a piece
    Game->>Game: Is that piece in the legal moves?
    alt Yes
        Game->>Socket: move_piece (pieceId)
        opt The landing square holds a defender and clash mode is on
            Engine-->>Game: clash_start (one round per defender)
            Note over Game: the chain applies the move on its last round
        end
        Engine-->>Game: piece_moved (with state_update, after any clash card)
        opt The move captured, and a clash settled it
            Note over Game: piece_moved and clash_result arrive together
            Game->>Game: card copy of the burst at the cell, CAPTURE_FX_CARD_OPACITY
            Note over Game: the card ends (its timer, or the engine's release)
            Game->>Game: CAPTURE_FX_AFTER_CARD_MS later the board's own burst fires
        end
        Game->>Game: Update the board
    else No
        Game->>User: Shake the piece ("can't move that one")
    end
```

## Logic Paths Summary

### Connect & Join Path
```
Game mounts with activeMatch
  ├── connectSocket(activeMatch.token)
  ├── on 'connect' → emit('join_game', gameId, color, userId, displayName)
  │    └── Hotseat: one join_game per local seat, then the host seat's identity join
  │        (a single socket controls every seat)
  ├── on 'game_joined' → dispatch into reducer
  ├── Hotseat turn rotation → re-emit join_game for the now-current seat so the
  │    engine's turn validation follows the seat actually being played
  └── Reconnect: on reconnect → re-emit join_game
```

The socket's reconnect budget is sized against the seat, not the transport (`src/socket.ts`):
`reconnectionAttempts: 16`, `reconnectionDelay: 2000`, `reconnectionDelayMax: 3000` and
`randomizationFactor: 0`, which is 2 s, then every 3 s, so the last attempt lands at 47 s — past the
45 s window a live seat keeps, so the client is still connected when `seat_expired` arrives instead of
going quiet. `window.focus` and `document.visibilitychange` re-run `socket.connect()` when the socket
is down, because a frozen or backgrounded tab stops counting attempts; the listener pair is removed in
the same effect's cleanup as the socket itself.

### Dice Roll Path
```
User clicks die (or taps the die at rest)
  └── emit('roll_dice'), tumble starts, brake window opens at the tap
       ├── tap the spinning die again after 200 ms → brake: land at 450..750 ms
       └── on 'dice_rolled' → the landing timer holds the value
            └── land at diceLandAt(start, valueAt, brakeAt)
                 └── dispatch { value, legalMoves, bonusRoll, currentTurn } → highlight pieces
```

### Piece Move Path
```
User clicks piece
  ├── Check if piece is in legalMoves for current dice
  │   ├── Yes → emit('move_piece', pieceId)
  │   │   └── on 'piece_moved' → dispatch → update board
  │   │        └── captured → capture burst on the landing cell
  │   │             └── clash settled the move → the card wears a dim copy
  │   │                  (CAPTURE_FX_CARD_OPACITY), the board's burst waits for the
  │   │                  card to go + CAPTURE_FX_AFTER_CARD_MS
  │   └── No → show invalid feedback
```

### Game End Path
```
on 'game_ended' { winner, resultDetail }
  ├── setLastResult({ winner, resultDetail, mode, playerCount, players })
  └── Open the ResultsModal overlay in-game
```

## Implementation Notes

- **The box under the board is two controls.** CONTROLS & SETTINGS (`gameExtra.controlsSettings`, cyan) opens `components/ControlsSettingsModal.tsx`: a 340 px popover on the `LegalModal` shell (backdrop click and Escape close, the same UI beeps) holding three key rows — dice/`spacebarKey`, select piece/`leftClickKey`, and the goal count as plain text — the auto-close row, and, since 32.1, the dock's own language and theme switches, mounted as a fourth host rather than forked: `LanguageSwitcher` takes `variant="tabs"` with the store's `lang`/`setLang`, and `ThemeSwitcher` takes no props. The retro audio switch — which reads `muted` from the page and calls `retroAudio.toggleMute()` back, labelled `game.audioOn` / `game.audioOff` — closes the column but is the popover's **footer, outside the scrolling body**: the rows cap themselves at `calc(100dvh - 2rem)` with `overflowY: auto`, because the second and third switches stand above the audio row, and the switch that unmutes a silent game is the one control that must not be scrolled for (its beep is played by the press, so a hidden row reads as dead). GAME RULES (`gameExtra.gameRulesBtn`, pink) resets `rulesPage` to 0 and opens the ten-page deck. Both sit in one flex row in COLUMN 2, below the board and outside the waiting-room gate, so they are there while the room waits and mid-game; that column is `order-1` when the layout stacks, so a tablet and a phone get the same pair under the board.
- **The room's channel is one card with two hosts** (`components/ChatCard.tsx`, M3). `ChatCard` renders the compact slot (the newest three lines plus `gameChat.openHint`) and, on press, the same card as a fixed overlay anchored to the slot's rectangle — the placement `LobbyChat` already used, because where the slot sits depends on the roster's overhang and the window height, and on `/game` the slot is *sized* by that same overhang: the roster above it is `calc(100% + rosterOverhang)`, 46 px past its grid track for the display names and 152 in a clash, where the Wall Breaker column rides along, and the slot's host takes the identical width rather than the track's own. Two windows stacked in one column end on one edge, so the feed does not sit short of the roster over it, and the overhang is read from the same expression the roster uses (`rosterOverhang`), so a clash room's wider panel widens both and no state draws them out of line. The host's wrapper is also what absorbs the stretched column's slack; the slot keeps `flex: 1 1 auto` inside it, so the plate's height, its pin on OPEN ⤢ and its overlay anchor are unchanged. The transcript, the composer (`n / 300`, UTF-16 like the server) and the one reserved status band live **inside** the card, so a `game_state` re-render cannot drop a draft; the card's height is a budget rather than a ceiling (19.3: `CHAT_CARD_HEIGHT`, 560, exported from the same file and imported by the dock), so the head and the band are drawn inside it and the transcript gives up the rows instead of the card growing under them; the lines live in `useChatFeed` (`hooks/useChatFeed.ts`, capped at 200) so a frame cannot drop a line. `chat:history` replaces the local copy on every join and rejoin (it is the authority, already cut for this seat), and `chat:message` is deduped by id at the seam — both rules are the hook's, which is also what the lobby's public channel drives, so the two surfaces cannot order or dedupe differently (19.8). A clash sets `inert`: the card stays open and readable, the composer is disabled and Escape is the only key it takes. The in-game name card is `ChatNameCard` (19.7), the same component the lobby mounts — ADD FRIEND in two presses, ACCEPT REQUEST, BLOCK through `ConfirmActionModal` — mounted with no `challenge` and reading a `useFriendships` snapshot the page owns, because the invite panel owns inviting in that context; its outcomes land in the transcript card's band, which stays open beneath it. The lobby imports the same head, feed, band and composer (it passes no glyph, so the head has no switch and shows only CLOSE), so both channels paint one transcript, show one `n / 300`, refuse at one length and reserve one band height.
- **The room's channel is named for what it is** (`locales/*.ts`, 20.3). `gameChat.title` reads `# ENCRYPTED COMMS` — the expanded card's head and its dialog label, and the default `titleKey` the room's card passes to `ChatCardHead` — and `gameChat.slotTitle` reads `ENCRYPTED COMMS`, on the compact plate's header row and as its accessible name. The pair is now the contrast the lobby's public channel was missing: `chat.title` stays `# HYPERSPACE COMMS`, this one is private by construction (only the seats in the room read it, and the transcript is stored with the match), and two of its own strings already said so — `gameChat.emptyDesc` ("only the pilots in this room read this channel") and `gameChat.scope` (`THIS MATCH ONLY`). The plate's header now says both words as well: that header row holds the title alone (`WINDOW_HEADER` is `flex justify-between` with a single child, and the occupancy column is the side panel's, portaled outside the card), so a second word costs only its own width — 15 characters at `--font-heading`'s 0.75rem come to roughly 105 px against a 250 to 310 px track, which leaves the untruncating header room to spare and lets a shut card name the channel as fully as an open one. `gameChat.inviteTitle` keeps its meaning (INVITE COMMS), `gameChat.scope` was left alone, and the lobby's two hard-coded `aria-label`s (`LobbyChat.tsx`) are the lobby's: a naming pass is not where a missing key gets fixed.
- **The room ends on one line, and the feed is what fills the last of it** (`pages/Game.tsx` + `components/ChatCard.tsx`, 20.4). The main grid is `alignItems: 'stretch'` rather than `'start'`, so every column takes the row's height instead of stopping at its own content; the left column adds `minHeight: 0` so it may be shorter than its children, and `ChatCard`'s slot row — the column's last child — takes `flex: 1 1 auto` with its own `minHeight: 0`, so it absorbs the slack. The plate's body already grows inside the window (`WINDOW_BODY`'s `grow`), which is what pins `gameChat.openHint` (`OPEN ⤢`) to the new bottom edge instead of leaving it in the middle of the panel; no state, no new control, and `data-chat-reserve` (19.3) is untouched because nothing here moves horizontally. The bottom lands on the **tallest column**: in practice the board column, whose board is capped at `min(650px, 66vh)` and which carries the CONTROLS & SETTINGS row below it, but a long recon log in column 3 wins late in a game and the plate follows it — the rule is that the plate fills whatever the row became, not that it is locked to the board, and a hard lock would need a `maxHeight` derived from the board's cap for a case that does not exist. In a waiting room the occupancy column stretches too, so a short friend list sits in a tall window and scrolls; that is the recorded trade against `alignItems: 'flex-start'` on the plate's own row, which unbalances the pair. The stacked layout is untouched: one column, one item per row, nothing to stretch to.
- **The invite panel is the waiting room's, and only its.** `InviteComms` lists the friends whose presence reads `online` **and** whose id is not in `view.players` (the room's own seat list, never a presence snapshot), and `Game.tsx` only passes it as `sidePanel` while `view.status === 'waiting'`, so it is gone when the match starts. Since 19.8 it is a panel rather than a column: portaled beside the open card, closed by default behind the card head's switch (beside CLOSE), and therefore it no longer hides at capacity — a full room lists nobody and says so. A friend mid-match in another room still reads `online` here and pressing invite is answered in the card's band in one press; hiding them would need a live signal the gate does not have yet.
- **The waiting room is the seats, the room rules and nothing else.** The seat rail, READY and the host-gated rules panel are the whole waiting screen: the room carries no friend list and no invite control. Challenging a friend into a PvP room is offered from the waiting room's INVITE COMMS panel and from the lobby chat's name card, and `/friends` mints a room directly, all three against `POST /api/friends/:friendId/invite` (the panel and the name card pass `{ gameId }`, the friends page passes `{ hostColor }`). The rail draws `railColors`: while the room waits it is `view.players` sorted by `SEAT_COLORS` — the room's own seats in turn order, one row per seat, so a 2-seat room holding green and blue shows those two and draws no phantom red (before the first engine frame, when `view.players` is still empty, it falls back to the canonical prefix for the room's seat count); once the match is live it is `SEAT_COLORS`, so an unseated corner still reads as an empty seat.
- **The socket effect depends on identity fields only.** The connect effect watches `activeMatch.gameId` and `.token`, never the whole `activeMatch` object. Changing `.mode`, `.color` or `.playerCount` after connect (a lobby seat or colour change) therefore cannot close and reopen the socket during the handshake. Long-lived handlers read the current value through `activeMatchRef`, so they never hold a stale `activeMatch`.
- **Duplicate-move guard.** `pendingMoveRef` is set as soon as `move_piece` is sent, and cleared when the server replies with `piece_moved` or a rejection. Without it, a second click on a piece that still looks legal, before the reply arrives, sends a duplicate `move_piece` that the engine rejects with "Invalid turn phase".
- **`canRoll` also requires `status === 'active'`.** This closes a short race condition (two operations competing in timing) at the end of a game: the winning move can leave the other roll inputs looking usable for one render before `game_ended` arrives, which would let a click through as a "Game not active" rejection.
- **Seat identity on the first render.** For a PvP (player versus player) joiner, `ck === view.myColor` can be out of date until the server replies: the engine assigns the seat through a `lobby_update`; the page turns that changed colour into a local `my_color_changed` action and a fresh `join_game`, both of which may happen after the first render, so the seat briefly appears to belong to someone else. The page also accepts a direct `playerMeta.username === user.username` match (the same check the active-game pilot card uses) so it recognises the seat immediately.
- **Refresh safety for cached matches.** After a browser refresh, a cached `activeMatch` can arrive with no `mode`. The page reads it again from `GET /api/games/mine`, so a hotseat or PvE (player versus environment) game can never be treated as a plain PvP rejoin.
- **"RETURN TO LOBBY" does not leave the match.** The control is drawn for every ended game, for a live online PvP, and (20.6) for a live PvE that holds an account's seat; a guest PvE has no account to list and a live hotseat keeps QUIT TO MENU as its only exit. It only navigates back to `/gamelobby`: it emits no `leave_game` and calls no exit endpoint. Unmounting the page drops the socket, which opens the engine's disconnect grace window for that seat — 45 s in PvP, one hour in a PvE simulation. Inside the window the lobby offers a **REJOIN** row for the room (`GET /api/games/rooms` → `mySeat: true`, and `GET /api/games/mine` still lists it), and the dock's banner names the mode it opens (`REJOIN PVE MATCH`, no room line, because a simulation mints no code); rejoining restores the same colour and pieces. Once the window expires the engine marks the seat `exited` and parks its pieces at `step = -1`; both endpoints then stop advertising the room to that player: the seat stays behind as a ghost, and the player is free to start or join another game.
- **`dice_rolled` names the roller, and the recon log is engine state** (`game/reducer.ts` + `pages/Game.tsx`). On the no-move and third-six-forfeit paths the engine advances `currentTurn` before it emits, so the event carries `color` — the seat that actually rolled — and the reducer writes the face onto that seat (`players[].lastRoll`), which is what the roster's `MiniDie` renders. Nothing is guessed from the turn any more, so a rejoined client shows the right face on the right seat. - **A frame never overtakes the roll it followed** (`pages/Game.tsx`). A `dice_rolled` is dispatched when its die lands (the `diceLandAt` instant, `DICE_ROLL_ANIM_MS` at most), not when it arrives, so the movable pieces do not light up mid-spin; every other frame is applied on arrival. Those two rules can cross. The engine emits a roll and, 4 ms later, a `turn_skipped` when the roller passes a turn whose only exit is token-priced (the bot's declined Wall Breaker exit), so the skip reached the reducer first and the older roll — which names the seat that rolled, still current when it left the engine — overwrote the turn the engine had already moved on to. The board parked on the bot, its declined exit still lit, and since no bot acts out of turn and the human was no longer the seat to move, the room sat frozen while the engine waited for the human's roll. `handleEngineEvent` now lands a pending die before it applies any other frame, so the reducer always takes the frames in arrival order; `piece_moved` is the one exception, because its walk is meant to start on the landed face and its own dispatch already waits for the landing. Nothing is lost on the way either: before this, a second roll landing inside the first one's window replaced `rollLandFnRef` and silently dropped the earlier frame.
- **Tapping a spinning die brakes it, and the brake never touches the value** (`pages/Game.tsx` + `components/Die.tsx`). One handler covers the die's three states: at rest it starts a roll, a roll this device started brakes once `DICE_BRAKE_ARM_MS` (200 ms) has passed, and everything else — another seat's roll, a tap inside the arming window, a roll already braked — is ignored. The clock opens at the tap that started the roll, not at the server's answer, so the window lasts the same whatever the round trip is, and `rollStartRef` stays null for a roll this device did not start, which is what keeps an opponent's or a bot's dice un-brakeable. The landing is `diceLandAt(start, valueAt, brakeAt)` in `game/timing.ts`: without a brake it is the full `DICE_ROLL_ANIM_MS` (750 ms), with one it is `max(450 ms from the start, 150 ms after the tap, 150 ms after the answer)` and never past the natural end — a roll can be shortened, never extended, and a slow answer wins over the shortened window rather than settling on a face nobody sent. The die slows onto the neighbouring face (`brakeTo ± 1`, never 0 or 7) and lands on the server's value, which the dispatch carries untouched. `piece_moved` then waits out what is LEFT of the spin (`rollLandAtRef`) instead of the whole constant, so a braked roll does not leave the walk sitting still for time the roll no longer has. The die is the tap surface and its button is never disabled — that is the requirement the brake needs — while the ROLL button keeps its disabled-while-spinning state, because a brake labelled ROLLING… would need new copy in three locales before it could mean anything.

The key box is 67×61 px with a 2 px border, measured from Atkinson Hyperlegible Bold at 54 px: the widest glyph (W, 47.7 px advance) and the tallest (Q, 39.7 px with descender) keep 2 mm (about 7.56 px) clearance on every side. Width is the advance plus 4 mm plus two borders; height is the line-box model plus two borders. The press bar is a 168 px track for 42 presses, so each press adds exactly 4 px and the bar is full only at the target.
- **The result card's scene is one scene, played twice, and its clock is derived from the card's own budget** (`game/ResultFx.tsx` + `game/ClashOverlay.tsx` + `common/src/timing.constants.ts`). Both outcomes are the same picture with the roles swapped, so `kind` decides nothing but whose colour is on the ink: the **winner** fires a beam across the lane, the beam strikes the **loser**, the field cracks under it, and after a ~600 ms beat an electrified cell slams closed on it from the winner's side. `capture` (the attacker won) fires the attacker's beam from its own front face — the attacker's LEFT, at x 138.5, because the attacker holds the right seat — at the defender's right flank at x 42.5, and closes the cell on the defender travelling that same vector; `repulse` (the defender won) fires the defender's beam from its RIGHT face at x 36.5 at the attacker's left flank at 132.5 and closes the cell on the attacker the same way. The two places never swap: the defender stands on the left at x 6.5 and the attacker on the right at 132.5 on any card and in any act, so a chain's rounds read as one continuous shot, and the winner always fires from its own front face. The pair's cast never changes — both pieces are drawn at the same two places whichever way the round went, because a clip stops being one clip if the cast changes — and the bars are the winner's colour, which cannot collide with the prisoner's, since the prisoner is the loser. They also face each other while they stand there: the defender looks right and the attacker looks left, in every act, through `PieceFigure`'s `pose` — a **face plate** turned 37° inside a head that does not move, one matrix (`CREW_TURN`'s squash 0.8 about x 50 plus its 6-unit slide), with the 70s visor travelling across a helmet shell that stays put and the 80s `PIECE_FACE_80` plate travelling across its own head. Nothing is mirrored: the 80s figures were redrawn carrying nothing, so a `left` figure and a `right` one differ only in where the face sits, and no gradient has to be reversed (`todo.md` [30](todo.md#rv30-set)). It costs the box nothing (0 of 32 posed renders leave the 100-unit box — `tools/80s/posecheck.py` — and on all 16 posed rows of both sets `tools/80s/neckcheck.py` reads the head's outline **0.0** and the face **±6.0**, so no head leaves its collar), which is why the cast's box, the wall constants and the shot did not have to move for the pose; the box's own widening to 36 units is 32.2's, and it re-pointed the seven values measured from it, the seats held at 24.5 and 150.5 so the boxes are x 6.5 to 42.5 and 132.5 to 168.5, a 90-unit gap; the same 24 drawings also ship as stills under `frontend/public/pieces` (one `.svg` each, and it is the canon: a `.png` is a raster *of* it, written only under `STILL_PNG=1`), written by the same script from the same tables, so a swap to sprites would not be a redraw. It is parameterised by one `n`, the wall's defender count (the defenders still standing, plus the one a success takes): a wall is drawn as **its defenders under the dome that encloses them** — `n` glyphs on a column that recedes and shrinks, the front box at x 108 and each further one 0.82 of the width, a third of its own box overlapped by the piece in front and 8.4 units higher, so the four boxes are 36, 29.5, 24.2 and 19.8 wide and the group spans x 59 to 144, lowest and right-most at the front because the shot arrives from the right. Over them, four **frozen** rings about x 85 on the ground line: 78.7 is the line fitted to the ink (the minimal semicircle over those four boxes is 74.88 about x 85, plus a 4 px pad at 1.75 px/unit and half a 3.0 stroke), and 84.2, 89.7 and 95.2 follow at a 5.5 pitch; `n` draws the innermost `n`, so the line fitted to the ink is always there and the shield hugs the group it protects. The strokes invert the pair's ladder — 2.2 inside to 3.2 out — because the outermost ring is both the face the shot meets and the line that glitches out, and a 5-unit stroke cannot live on a 5.5-unit pitch anyway; all four paint in front of the glyphs, since an enclosing curve never crosses one, and the count reads twice: `n` glyphs and `n` rings. The dome sits outside the column rather than in the middle of it — the outer rim at 180.2 is 15.8 units clear of the attacker's bay and the inner rim is at 6.3, so the two outer rings leave the frame on the left. The shot is a stub in the right-hand bay — the attacker stands in frame at x 196 to 232, on the same ground line, outside the dome — so a capture runs 202 to the face at 173.1 (the outermost ring's own right flank at the strike height, `STRIKE_Y` 84, which is the line every shot in the game travels on) and a repulse comes back off that same face to the attacker's flank at 196, the one place in the game where a shot reverses; the face is the one mark that moves with the count — 167.2 at three rings, 161.1 at two, 155.0 at one — so a wall's beam is 28.9 units on a capture and 22.9 on a repulse at four rings, the beam travels its own width rather than `BEAM_TRAVEL_PX`, and keeps the act's 247 ms arrival, so a wall reads as a punch at close range. One success subtracts one defender **and** one line: the struck defender's fade begins on the 418 subtraction and completes at 880, and the outermost ring — the face — glitches twice and is gone by 1000. A wall's attack act is 900 ms longer than the pair's (`SCENE_WALL_BEAT_MS` 900, so `SCENE_WALL_ATTACK_MS` 1600): the dome takes the hit at 260, blobs over to its peak at 700 (1.04 wide and 0.97 tall, scaled about the ground under its centre so the rim travels and the column's feet stay put), resolves by 880, loses the face ring between 880 and 1000, and then **holds** for the last 600 — the stillness a 1v1 card gets from the jail's own tail, which a wall cannot borrow while its shield is the thing that was hit. Everything inside the head of the act is untouched by the extra 900: the beam still arrives at 247, the flare still peaks at 323, the line is still subtracted at 418, and the four marks that are pinned there keep `fx(name)`'s 700 while the three wall marks take `wallFx(name)`'s 1600 and `wPct`. A wall's act then **clears** at 1600 — the one cast change in the sequence — and the jail act re-presents the pair on the pair stage, so the cell always closes on the pair stage, the wall's own prisoner included (`fxWallClear` out at 1600, `fxCastIn` in 96 ms later, `forwards`-filled so the pair holds opacity 0 for the whole attack act). At `n === 1` the wall is a total breach, the last defender and its line going exactly as any other does — the last defender is not a special case — and then, after that first clear, the **cloud act**: a mushroom cloud (a veiled stem and a head of three puffs, one group scaling up out of the ground line, plus a flat blast ring that races out and fades in the act's first 500 ms) grows where the dome stood, on its centre at x 85, the sentence naming the winner pulses twice inside it, and the frame is cleared a second time as the jail's pair arrives. So a breach card is attack + cloud + jail — three acts and two cuts — the longest card there is (`CLASH_BREACH_RESULT_MS` 4200: a wall's 1600 attack, the cloud and the jail, where a wall that does not break is `CLASH_WALL_RESULT_MS` 3800) and the only one with no read window after the scene, because its sentence lives inside the cloud act instead: it starts 400 ms in (`CLOUD_TEXT_LEAD_MS`, 2000 ms of the card) and finishes exactly with the act. The sentence is `<name> breaks through the wall !!!` in all three locales, the name resolved in `ClashOverlay` from the winner's seat (`displayName`, then `username`, then the colour word, which is the fallback the headline uses too) so `ResultFx` still draws a finished string and never sees a player, and its size is computed from the string's own length — `max(11, min(27, floor(224 / (0.62 * length))))`, with `textLength` 224 and `lengthAdjust='spacingAndGlyphs'` when even the floor overflows — and the halo and hairline it was tuned with (6 and 0.75 units) scale with it, so a long name stays open instead of drowning in glow. `ClashOverlay` picks the kind from fields the frame already carries — `result.attacker ?? clash.attacker` and `winner === attacker` — with `totalRounds` and the remaining-defender count feeding `n`, and renders the stage above the headline keyed on `cardId`, so each round of a chain mounts its own scene and fires its own sound exactly once. `RESULT_FX_MS` (1900) is now the SUM of its two acts — `SCENE_ATTACK_MS` (700) and `SCENE_JAIL_MS` (1200) — and `RESULT_HEADLINE_READ_MS` (1000) and the card's own `CLASH_RESULT_MS` (2900) with it, all declared once in `common/src/timing.constants.ts` and copied to each tree's `__shared__/timing.constants` — not `game/timing.ts`, and no longer re-exported by the engine — so the card, the reported hold, the freeze and the chain's next round move together from one edit. A wall's attack act is that act plus `SCENE_WALL_BEAT_MS` (`SCENE_WALL_ATTACK_MS` 1600), which is what makes a wall's card `CLASH_WALL_RESULT_MS` (3800) and a breach's `CLASH_BREACH_RESULT_MS` (4200); `SCENE_CLOUD_MS` (1400) is the third act a breach adds, and `CLASH_TEXT_ONLY_MS` (1400) is the card with the acts switched off. Each element's `animation` string is budgeted in the act that owns it — `fx(name)` for the attack's 700 (the marks pinned to its head, on a wall too), `wallFx(name)` for a wall's three marks over its 1600, `cloudFx(name)` for the cloud's 1400 after the attack act, `jailFx(name)` for the jail's 1200 — so nothing can outlive its act, and the keyframes are written in milliseconds and converted to that act's percentages (`aPct`, `wPct`, `jPct`, `cPct`). The jail is the second act everywhere and the THIRD on a breach, so its start is one number (`jailStartMs`, 700 on a pair, 1600 on a wall, 3000 on a breach) and everything that belongs to it is derived from it: its own animations, the cast-in of the pair, and the slam's beat. The read window is not an act: `readFx(name, startMs)` takes its start, which on a breach is 400 ms into the cloud (2000) — and a breach has no read window after the scene at all. The cell's two beats are read in the JAIL act's own clock (`CAGE_START_IN_JAIL_MS` 136 and `CAGE_LAND_IN_JAIL_MS` 421 of its 1200, i.e. 836 and 1121 ms of a pair's card, the instants the one-gesture card played) and the sound table's snap is *derived* from the jail's start plus that landing (`jailStartMs + CAGE_LAND_IN_JAIL_MS`), so the snap is heard on the slam rather than 191 ms before it — 1121 ms of a pair's card, 2021 of a wall's and 3421 of a breach; the soundtrack is per act rather than per host (25.5): `playLaserBeam` at 0, `playBarrierPuncture` once per layer 40 ms apart from 260, `playBreachBloom` at low weight on the flare's 323 ms peak, `playShatterTick` at the 418 ms glitch, `playBarrierDeflect` on a wall repulse and `playAttackFailed` on a 1v1 one, the full `playBreachBloom` opening the cloud at 1600, and `playCageSlam` on the cell at 1121 (2021 on a wall, 3421 on a breach). A non-clash capture reuses the same component as the opt-in flash card (top-centre over the board, the same soundtrack since 25.5 — both hosts mount the same acts, so the flash card keeps its laser), at the clash stage's own 0.8: `CLASH_STAGE_MAX_PX` 420 and `FLASH_CARD_STAGE_MAX_PX` 336 are the two ends of one ratio (48vw of 60vw, and 192 of 240 units, both at 1.75 px per unit), and `FLASH_CARD_VIEWBOX` `0 15 192 144` is a 0.8-scaled crop of the same 4:3 frame, so nothing about either clip is rescaled. The one gate on every act of every card is the room's `captureFx`, and it reaches this component as the overlay's `animations` prop: off means no stage, no scene sound and a text-only card of `CLASH_TEXT_ONLY_MS`, whose receipt asks for 1600 instead of 3100 — and the receipt's number is always the card's own, through `clashCardMs` in `game/timing.ts` (which the overlay's timer and `Game.tsx` both call), so a wall asks for 4000 and a breach 4400 and nothing has to repeat the test. `prefers-reduced-motion` no longer participates anywhere in the stack — the OS hook and the `fxFade` cross-fade are deleted, in both `ResultFx` and `ClashOverlay`, because every fact the scene carries is also in the card's text (25.7). `aria-hidden`, no input, and `pointerEvents: 'none'` on the stage.
- **A mid-clash key switch is scheduled on the SERVER clock, and the letter is read, never guessed** (`game/ClashOverlay.tsx` + `game/reducer.ts` + `pages/Lobby.tsx` + `pages/Game.tsx`). The room rule `clashKeyShift` (0, 1 or 2) makes the required letter change mid-race. The engine draws one schedule per side (`attackerKeys`/`defenderKeys`, element 0 being the legacy `attackerKey`/`defenderKey`) plus the absolute instants (`switchAt`) and stamps `serverNow` on the frame; the reducer turns that stamp into ONE offset per clash (`serverNow - Date.now()` at receipt), and the overlay schedules a timer per switch against it. The render tick is 250 ms and could be a quarter second late, so the tick never drives a switch: it only supplies the derived index, and the timer sets the exact one. The badge flips on Y with the face edge-on exactly AT the instant (`CLASH_SWITCH_FLIP_MS` before and after), the touch pad re-scrambles its four circles (`drawPad` also keys on the switch index, so a letter the schedule is about to ask for cannot already be under the thumb) and lands them on X with a `CLASH_SWITCH_PAD_STAGGER_MS` stagger, and a banner (`game.clashKeyShiftWarn`, `CLASH_SWITCH_WARN_MS` ahead) never names the new letter — since 4.7 that banner is a cue in its own right: it pulses (`clash-switch-warn--on`, one 400 ms `pulse-glow` breath inside the stylesheet's existing motion gate, so `prefers-reduced-motion` leaves it simply lit) and the `playCyberReject()` sting sounds WITH the warning, 400 ms before the flip, because the flip already carries the banner, the pad re-scramble and the round's own tone. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the flip is a cross-fade at the same instants. Both schedules ride the one room-wide frame on purpose: a hotseat socket owns both seats and needs both, and a client cannot press for a seat it does not own. The count is host-only in both room-rules panels (a checkbox plus a `1 / 2` select, disabled unless Clash Mode is on, keeping its own count so re-enabling restores it), and the reducer reads the canonical value from `state_update`/`modifiers_updated`.



- **The rules deck is the one rules UI, and its last four pages are the room-rule copy.** `Game.tsx` renders ten pages inside the shared `CyberModal` (`isSystemModalOpen`), tabbed by `rulesTab0..9` and advanced with NEXT PAGE until the last page's START PLAYING closes it: 0-5 the classic deck (objective, rolling a 6, captures, stars, blockades, home lane), 6 clash, 7 hardcore, 8 free exit, 9 the Wall Breaker. Every page draws its copy from `gameExtra.rulesPage*`; page 0 also uses `gameExtra.rulesCombatControls` / `rulesRollDiceLabel` / `rulesSelectWarpLabel` / `spacebarKey` / `leftClickKey`. All of them live in the three locales, and the deck is the only rules surface: there is no diagram component and no other rules modal. The clash page's extra line (`rulesPage6Chain`) is the live chain rule: landing on a wall is one clash per defender, each round a fresh key race, which is the `captureCount` the board shows as a ×R badge and the overlay as a ROUND n / total pill. The hardcore page states both halves of `noSafeZones`: the stars stop protecting anywhere on the track (the four start squares are in the engine's `SAFE_TRACK_POSITIONS`, so they lose their immunity too; only the home lane still shields a piece), and a stack on a star - exempt in a standard room - blocks the path. The breakable-wall gate is PER SQUARE (`wallIsAttackable`): clash mode plus a token in hand opens a wall on an ordinary square, while a wall parked on a star needs the hardcore flag as well, because a protected star neither blocks the path nor can be attacked. The free-exit page notes that a 6 keeps its bonus roll and that the exit is an ORDINARY move from step 0 finishing on the roll itself (`to` = the roll: a 5 lands on step 5, a 6 on step 6): a wall on its own track - its landing square included - blocks it exactly as it blocks any other move, and only a wall on the square it LANDS on can be fought (clash mode, one clash per defender). Breaking the wall it LANDS on is the token's job, so the Wall Breaker page (9) carries the rest: every seat is dealt `STARTING_WALL_BREAKER_TOKENS` (1) at game start (`wallBreakerTokens`, shown in the token column beside the roster, with `WALL_BREAKER_TOKEN_CAP` glyph slots lit once per token in hand), the token-priced exit (`spendsWallBreakerToken`, marked with `WALL_BREAKER_GLYPH` on the piece only while the seat is armed) is offered only when the plain exit is illegal for that roll AND the roll lands exactly on a wall, so a piece never carries two exits on one roll and a tap can never spend a token by accident. The wall break is a LANDING, never a crossing: a wall further along the corridor stays out of reach, and the roll still has to reach the wall's square exactly. The token is spent on the ATTEMPT - inside the game lock against the fresh state, so a replayed emit finds the move snapshot cleared and cannot spend a second, and a repulsed chain keeps it spent - while a clash won against exactly one defender pays one back, up to `WALL_BREAKER_TOKEN_CAP` (three). A turn the wall break is holding is passed with SKIP, which keeps the token in hand. Every won chain credits one `clashWallBattlesWon` when its landing square stood a wall (the `achTearDownThisWall` tally, two in one game).
- **Every pilot card shows an avatar, bots included.** A seat with an account passes its `userId`, `hasAvatarPhoto` and `avatarStyle` to `UserAvatar`, which asks for the photo only when one is known to exist and otherwise renders the generated DiceBear avatar seeded from the seat's name. A bot has no account, so `seatAvatarName()` seeds it with the assistant name the lobby gave that seat (`avatarSeed()` in `utils/botName.ts`), which is the seed the lobby table and the results invoice reuse, and `isBot` keeps the photo request off. **In hotseat, only the host's seat can show a photo.** The engine leaves `userId` undefined for every local seat (one socket drives them all), so they take the generated path, seeded from the name typed into the lobby; the host's own seat is the exception, because it is the seat this device joined the match with (`activeMatch.color`, always slot 0). That card reads the store's `/me` facts instead (`user.id`, `user.hasAvatarPhoto`, `user.avatarStyle`), so the host keeps their uploaded photo, and it seeds the generated avatar with the account username, which is the seed the navbar and the lobby host row already use. No other local seat issues a photo request.
- **One duplex star marks all eight safe squares** (`components/Board.tsx`). The four start cells and the four pre-start cells (`SAFE_TRACK_POSITIONS`, mirrored from `board-mapper.ts`) render the same `SafeStar` at 44% of the cell: a white core in a black outline, because no single color clears all five fills the star has to sit on. The two layers cover each other's blind spots — white measures 20.2:1 on the near-black cell but only 1.3–1.4:1 on green/yellow/blue, where the black ring measures 14.9–16.6:1; on the red start cell white is 3.8:1 and the ring 5.6:1, both above the 3:1 non-text threshold. A single hue was tried first and lost: `#b14aff` read pale on the pink start cell (same-hue clash) and the deep-indigo `#3c0fe6` candidate measured 2.3:1 against both the dark cell and the pink start, so it sank into the track. The black ring also matches the pieces that stand on the same cells, which are outlined in near-black (`ink`, `#0a0716`) in both sets. The `noSafeZones` room rule withholds every one of the eight, start cells included. Before this, the starts drew a white star unconditionally and only the other four were rule-aware, so turning safe zones off hid 4 of 8 and left the set looking like two different features. A start cell keeps its owner tint with the rule on, because the tint names the SEAT while the star names the protection.
- **One helper builds the results handoff.** `toResultPlayers(players, winner?)` maps the live roster onto `LastResult.players` for all three builders (`game_ended`, the abandoned fallback, and `endGame`), so every path carries the same avatar facts to the invoice. It reads `activeMatchRef`/`userRef` rather than the render's own values, because the socket handlers outlive the render that created them. The optional `winner` records that seat as four pieces: the winning move can still be mid-animation when `game_ended` arrives, so the view lags the final count. Hotseat's host seat gets the store's `/me` identity stamped onto it (the same `activeMatch.color` test as the pilot card), which is what lets `ResultsModal` recognise the host's row by `userId` and show their photo. A bot row keeps its raw `bot-<color> (<assistant>)` label, because the invoice localizes it for display and seeds the generated avatar from it. A seat whose engine status is `exited` is marked `abandoned` on its row: the invoice keeps its name and gives it no podium place and no rank badge, so a dropped player who kept a pieces-home count can never be crowned over the real winner.
- **A card can come from the database, not from a frame (M1).** The frame that ends a match is addressed to the sockets still inside the room, so a seat that was away when the room ended gets nothing. `Game.tsx` therefore asks `GET /api/game/:id/result` on the three signals that mean "this room will not be joined": a mount whose match is already terminal, a refused `join_game` (`MATCH_GAME_NOT_FOUND`), and `reconnect_failed` after the socket's 16 attempts. A `finished` answer sets `matchOverRef`, so `join_game` is never re-emitted over a decided match, and renders the card from the row (`rowAsLastResult`: the seat snapshot gives the roster and the abandoned flags, and a room that did not finish names no champion, so `winner` is `null`). `pending` leaves the socket alone; `unknown` keeps whatever card the page already holds. An abandoned card built locally no longer names the seat that held the turn as the winner either.
